About

I am a PhD student in Bioinformatics at Université Paris Cité (INSERM U1133, supervised by Pr. Olivier Taboureau). My thesis asks which environmental chemicals raise breast cancer risk, and answers it by integrating two kinds of data that are rarely analysed together: transcriptomic responses to chemical exposure and high-content cell imaging. The approach is comparative — confronting the signature a compound leaves in a cell line with the deregulation measured in tumours, then ranking suspected carcinogens by how closely they resemble those already established, so that a chemical can be flagged without an animal test.

Prior to my PhD, I worked as a Research Engineer at École Polytechnique (Laboratoire d’Optique et Biosciences, 2021–2023), where I developed custom Graph Neural Networks to predict protein-ligand binding affinity and conducted structure-based virtual screening campaigns targeting ThyX inhibitors. I was also awarded the X-Grant prize through the X-Novation entrepreneurial program.

My earlier research at INSERM covered systems biology, network toxicology, olfactory receptor–molecule relationships, and the application of deep learning to multi-target pharmacology.

Code & Data

Every published analysis is released as a reproducible repository: versioned download scripts, frozen environments, and a stated list of what the work does not show.

GitHub · Python, R · MIT
breast-cancer-risk-assessment-transcriptomics

Ranks chemicals as candidate breast cancer risk factors by comparing their transcriptional footprint in MCF7 (LINCS L1000) with the deregulation measured between 1,092 TCGA-BRCA tumours and GTEx normal breast tissue. Includes a similarity threshold calibrated against a null distribution of random profiles, and WGCNA co-expression networks linking compounds to gene modules.

View repository
GitHub · Python · MIT · Zenodo
mammary-carcinogenicity-cellpainting

Morphological profiling of JUMP Cell Painting data and a Guilt-By-Association scoring framework that ranks suspected mammary carcinogens, applied prospectively to endocrine-disruptor candidates from EDLists. Five similarity metrics, three feature representations, leave-one-out validation and a feature-selection stability diagnostic.

View repository
